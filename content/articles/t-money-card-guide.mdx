If you do exactly one piece of transit homework before Korea, make it this: get a T-money card within an hour of landing. It's a small rechargeable card that pays for subways, buses and taxis in Seoul **and almost everywhere else in the country**, plus convenience-store purchases when you don't feel like fishing out cash.

## Before you start

Two things trip people up, so let's clear them first:

1. **The card price and the balance are separate.** Paying ₩3,000 for the card gets you a card with ₩0 on it. You still need to load money.
2. **Cash is king for top-ups.** Foreign credit cards usually can't reload a T-money card. Keep some won on you for charging it.

## What you need

- About **₩2,500–4,000** for the card itself (plain designs are cheapest; character and collab designs cost a little more)
- Cash for your first top-up — **₩10,000** is a comfortable start for a few days of subway rides
- Nothing else. No ID, no registration, no app.

## Step-by-step

1. **Find a convenience store.** GS25, CU, 7-Eleven or Emart24 — there's one at the airport and roughly one on every block after that. The cards hang near the register; just point if in doubt.
2. **Buy the card, then ask to top up.** Say "충전해 주세요" (*chungjeon-hae juseyo* — "please charge it") or simply hand over the card and cash. Subway station machines also do top-ups and have an English menu.
3. **Tap in, tap out.** On the subway, tap the card reader at the gate on the way in *and* out. On buses, tap the reader by the front door when boarding — and tap again at the rear door when getting off if you plan to transfer.
4. **Watch your balance.** The gate display shows your remaining balance every time you tap out. When it dips below ₩1,550 (one subway base fare), top up.

## What it costs

- **Card:** ₩2,500–4,000 one-time (as of 2026)
- **Subway base fare in Seoul:** ₩1,550 with the card vs ₩1,650 for a single-ride ticket — the card saves ₩100 every ride before you even count transfers
- **Top-ups:** any amount from ₩1,000 to ₩90,000 per charge

The real money-saver is the **transfer discount**: tap off a bus or subway and onto the next leg within 30 minutes, and the system treats it as one journey instead of charging a fresh base fare. Single-ticket riders never get this.

## Common problems (and fixes)

- **"I forgot to tap out of the bus."** You'll be charged as if you rode to the end of the route, and you lose the transfer discount. Make tapping out a habit.
- **"The gate rejected my card."** Nine times out of ten it's an empty balance. Top-up machines are inside every station, before the gates.
- **"Can I share one card with my partner?"** No — one card per person, because the system tracks each traveler's transfers. Kids' and youth discount cards exist but require registration.

## Good to know

- The same card works in Busan, Daegu, Daejeon, Gwangju and most intercity buses — buy once, use nationwide.
- Taxis accept T-money, though drivers are just as happy with credit cards.
- Convenience stores accept T-money as payment, which is the classic way to burn a leftover balance on your last day.
