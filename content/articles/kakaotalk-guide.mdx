If you take one thing from your first week in Korea, make it this: download KakaoTalk. Koreans call it 카톡 (*katok* — "KakaoTalk," the everyday nickname), and roughly everyone here uses it. It's not just how friends chat — it's how your landlord confirms your move-in date, how a shop replies about a reservation, and the account that quietly powers KakaoPay, Kakao T taxis, KakaoBank and KakaoMap. This guide gets you from install to confidently messaging a stranger about an apartment.

## Before you start

KakaoTalk is more like an operating system for daily life than a chat app. One Kakao account logs you into a whole family of services, so the account you make now is the one you'll use for taxis, payments and banking later.

You **can** sign up with a foreign phone number — chat works on any number that can receive an SMS. But the moment you want to pay for a cab in the app, split a bill, or use KakaoBank, you'll usually need a **Korean phone number** plus a Korean bank or card and identity verification. That's why most people pair KakaoTalk with getting a local SIM. If you haven't sorted your number yet, see our [prepaid SIM guide](/phone-internet/korea-prepaid-sim-guide) or, for longer stays, [a real Korean phone plan](/phone-internet/korea-phone-plan-residents).

## What you need

- A smartphone (iOS or Android) with the App Store or Google Play.
- A **phone number that can receive an SMS verification code** — Korean or foreign.
- WiFi or mobile data.
- Optional but recommended: a Korean number, ARC and Korean bank account if you want KakaoPay and the paid services.

## Step-by-step

1. **Download the app.** Search "KakaoTalk" in the App Store (iPhone) or Google Play (Android) and install the official app by Kakao Corp. It's free.
2. **Start sign-up and enter your phone number.** Open the app, tap **New to Kakao? / Sign up**, and enter your mobile number. KakaoTalk sends a code by SMS.
3. **Enter the verification code.** Type in the code from the text message to confirm the number. This step is why a working SIM matters — no SMS, no account.
4. **Set your email and password.** These become your Kakao Account login, used across every Kakao service, so pick something you'll remember.
5. **Choose a display name and profile photo.** This is what friends see. You can change it anytime.
6. **Set a Kakao ID.** Go to the settings (gear) menu and set a **카카오톡 아이디** (*Kakao ID*) — a unique username. This lets people add you **without** your phone number. Highly recommended.
7. **Add your first friends.** Open the **Friends** tab and use any of these:
   - **Contact sync:** allow KakaoTalk to read your phone contacts and it auto-matches anyone already on Kakao.
   - **QR code:** tap the person icon / **Add friend**, then scan a friend's QR code (or show yours for them to scan).
   - **Kakao ID search:** tap **Add friend → search by ID** and type their Kakao ID.
8. **Start a chat.** Tap a friend to open a **1:1 chat**, or use **Add friend → create a group chat** to make a group. Everything below — calls, files, translation — lives inside a chat.

## What it costs

KakaoTalk itself is **free**: messages, voice calls, video calls and file sharing all run over WiFi or data at no charge. The things that cost money are optional:

- **Premium emoticons/stickers** — one-off purchases, often a few thousand won.
- **선물하기** (*seonmul-hagi* — "gifting") — sending a real gift like a café voucher; you pay the item's price.
- **카카오페이** (*Kakao Pei* — "KakaoPay") — you only pay for what you actually buy or transfer.

To send money or gifts you'll need KakaoPay set up, which for foreigners generally means a **Korean number, a Korean bank or card, and identity verification** tied to your ARC. It's worth doing once you're settled — see how it compares to other options in [Korean banking apps compared](/money-banking/korean-banking-apps-compared).

## Common problems (and fixes)

- **"I never got the SMS code."** Check signal, confirm the number is correct with country code, and request the code again. If your foreign number can't receive Korean SMS reliably, use a Korean SIM to register.
- **"A shop/landlord asked for my KakaoTalk but I don't want to give my number."** Send them your **Kakao ID** or your **QR code** instead. Businesses often prefer a **Channel** (a business account) — just search the shop's name in the search bar and message the channel.
- **"Strangers keep adding me."** In **Settings → Friends**, turn **off** "Add friends by phone number." After that, people can only add you via your Kakao ID or QR code — much better for privacy.
- **"I can't read the Korean messages."** KakaoTalk has built-in translation: long-press a message bubble and look for **Translate**. If it isn't there, open **Settings** and search "translation" to turn it on — Kakao occasionally moves the toggle between app versions.
- **"KakaoPay won't verify me."** A working chat account doesn't guarantee payments. Those flows depend on your number, bank, card and identity verification, so finish your ARC and Korean bank account first.

## Good to know

**Open Chats are Korea's secret layer.** 오픈채팅 (*open-chaeting* — "Open Chat") lets you join public or semi-public group rooms **without** being friends or sharing your number — think expat communities, neighborhood groups, hobby clubs, apartment-hunting rooms. Find them via the **Open Chat** tab or a shared link/QR code, and you can use a room-only nickname.

**Businesses live here too.** Many small shops, clinics, movers and landlords will only reply on KakaoTalk, not email or phone. Sending a polite Korean opener like 안녕하세요, 문의드립니다 (*annyeonghaseyo, munui-deurimnida* — "hello, I have a question") goes a long way, and the built-in translator handles the replies.

**Calls and files.** Free voice and video calls work in any chat, and you can send photos, documents and your live location — handy when a taxi or delivery driver needs to find you.

**Privacy basics.** Set your Kakao ID and QR as your default "handle," keep "add by phone number" off if you value privacy, and consider turning on **two-step verification** in settings to protect the account that now unlocks your money and rides.
