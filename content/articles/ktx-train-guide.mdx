Korea's KTX (Korea Train eXpress) turns Seoul-to-Busan into a comfortable 2-and-a-bit-hour ride at up to 300 km/h — no security line, no baggage fees, and a seat you can actually work in. Booking is genuinely easy in English once you know which app to use, and 2026 brought a big change: SRT and KTX are now one system.

## Before you start

A few things to know before you book:

- **One app now.** Since **September 1, 2026**, SRT merged into KTX and the two old apps (KORAIL Talk and the SRT app) became a single **KORAIL+** app. Book everything there or on the official English site.
- **Book early for weekends and holidays.** Popular Seoul–Busan trains and any travel around Chuseok or Lunar New Year sell out. Weekday off-peak seats are usually available same-day.
- **Know your Seoul station.** Most KTX trains leave from **Seoul Station** (서울역); some also stop at **Yongsan** (용산). Former SRT trains mainly leave from **Suseo** (수서) in the south of the city. Check which station your train uses — they are far apart.

## What you need

- A passport or ID for the booking name (not checked on board for domestic trips, but keep your ticket).
- An international credit card — KORAIL+ and korail.com/global accept foreign cards.
- Your phone, to hold the QR e-ticket. No printing required.
- Optional: a **Korea Rail Pass (KR Pass)** if you plan several long trips in a few days — it can beat buying point-to-point tickets. Worth doing the math for a multi-city trip.

## Step-by-step

1. **Open KORAIL+ or go to korail.com/global.** Set the language to English.
2. **Enter your trip.** Departure station, arrival station, date and number of passengers.
3. **Pick a train.** The results list shows departure time, duration and price. Fewer-stop trains are faster; check the arrival time, not just the departure.
4. **Choose class and seat.** Select Standard (일반실) or First Class (특실), then use the seat map to pick window or aisle and, importantly, **travel direction** — you can choose a forward-facing seat.
5. **Pay.** Enter your card details. You'll get a booking confirmation and a **QR e-ticket** in the app.
6. **Board.** Arrive 10–15 minutes early, find your car and seat numbers on the platform signs, and just walk on — there is no gate scan or security. Conductors may check the QR on board.

## What it costs

Fares are distance-based and fixed (no surge pricing). Reference prices for the flagship route:

- **Seoul → Busan, standard class:** about **₩59,800** one way.
- **Seoul → Busan, first class (특실):** about **₩83,700** — roughly 40% more, with wider 2-plus-1 seating and lounge access at major stations.
- **Shorter hops** cost proportionally less: Seoul → Daejeon or Seoul → Dongdaegu are cheaper.
- **A 10% fare cut** on existing KTX routes is being phased in over three years following the 2026 merger, so expect prices to ease slightly.

Booking a few days ahead can unlock small early-bird discounts on some trains. If you're weighing the train against a cheaper, slower ride, see [KTX vs express bus](/getting-around/ktx-vs-express-bus).

## Common problems (and fixes)

- **My foreign card is declined.** Use the KORAIL+ app or korail.com/global (the English channels are built for foreign cards); if it still fails, buy at a station ticket machine or staffed counter, which accept most cards.
- **The train sold out.** Try a nearby departure time, a train with one more stop, or first class — it often has seats left when standard is full. Same-day standing-room tickets exist on some routes.
- **I booked from the wrong station.** Seoul, Yongsan and Suseo are all separate stations across the city. Double-check the departure station before you pay, and give yourself time to reach it.
- **Refunds and changes.** You can cancel or change through the app; refund fees rise the closer you get to departure, so change plans early. Cancelling well ahead is usually free or near-free.

## Good to know

- **Luggage is easy.** Standard rolling suitcases fit the overhead racks; oversized bags go in the luggage shelves at the ends of each car. There are no weight fees or check-in — just carry it on.
- **Seats have power.** Outlets sit near the window seats, so a window seat is best if you want to charge a laptop. First class has 2-plus-1 seating and a bit more room.
- **Facilities.** Trains have restrooms, a snack vending area on many services, and quiet, smooth Wi-Fi-friendly cars. First class adds free lounge access at stations like Seoul, Busan, Daejeon and Dongdaegu.
- **Getting to the station.** Seoul Station connects to the subway and the AREX from the airport — see the [Seoul subway guide](/getting-around/seoul-subway-guide) and the [essential apps guide](/first-72-hours/essential-apps-korea) for navigation. A [T-money card](/getting-around/t-money-card-guide) covers the subway leg to and from the station.
