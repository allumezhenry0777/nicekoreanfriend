You don't need Korean to travel Korea — but ten grams of Korean buys a kilogram of goodwill. These twenty phrases are the actual working set, ordered by how often you'll fire them.

## How we chose

Each phrase had to be (1) short enough to memorize, (2) usable dozens of times per trip, and (3) understandable even with imperfect pronunciation. This is a toolkit, not a lesson.

## The list

### The big five

1. **안녕하세요** — *annyeong-haseyo* — "Hello." Universal: entering shops, meeting anyone, any time of day. Pair with a small nod.
2. **감사합니다** — *gamsahamnida* — "Thank you." The single highest-yield phrase in Korea. A casual alternative you'll hear: 고맙습니다 (*gomapseumnida*).
3. **주세요** — *juseyo* — "Please give me ___." The Swiss Army phrase: point at anything + "juseyo" = a complete, polite sentence. 이거 주세요 (*igeo juseyo*) = "this one, please."
4. **네 / 아니요** — *ne / aniyo* — "Yes / no." Note: *ne* can also be a general "mm-hm, I hear you."
5. **죄송합니다** — *joesong-hamnida* — "I'm sorry / excuse me (apology)." Bumped someone, blocking the door, general repair work.

### Restaurants and cafes

6. **저기요!** — *jeogiyo* — "Excuse me!" (calling staff). Not rude — it's the standard summons where there's no [call bell](/food-dining/how-to-order-korean-restaurant).
7. **얼마예요?** — *eolmayeyo* — "How much is it?"
8. **잘 먹겠습니다** — *jal meokgesseumnida* — "I'll eat well" (before eating). The politeness bomb — deploy when someone treats or serves you.
9. **잘 먹었습니다** — *jal meogeosseumnida* — "I ate well" (after eating, to staff on the way out). Restaurant owners visibly brighten.
10. **덜 맵게 해 주세요** — *deol maepge hae juseyo* — "Less spicy, please." Insurance for the [beginner dishes](/food-dining/korean-dishes-for-beginners).
11. **포장해 주세요** — *pojang-hae juseyo* — "To go / takeout, please."
12. **물 주세요** — *mul juseyo* — "Water, please" (though water is usually [self-serve](/food-dining/how-to-order-korean-restaurant)).

### Getting around and shopping

13. **화장실 어디예요?** — *hwajangsil eodiyeyo* — "Where's the bathroom?" Arguably phrase #1 in importance-per-use.
14. **영어 하세요?** — *yeongeo haseyo?* — "Do you speak English?" Politer than launching into English cold.
15. **이거 있어요?** — *igeo isseoyo?* — "Do you have this?" (showing a photo). The shopping cheat code.
16. **카드 돼요?** — *kadeu dwaeyo?* — "Do you take card?" (Answer: [almost always yes](/money-banking/cash-vs-card-korea).)
17. **여기서 세워 주세요** — *yeogiseo sewo juseyo* — "Please stop here" (taxi).

### Social and emergency

18. **괜찮아요** — *gwaenchanayo* — "It's okay / I'm fine / no thanks." Astonishingly versatile: declining a bag at the register, reassuring someone who bumped you, waving off seconds.
19. **한국어 못해요** — *hangugeo motaeyo* — "I can't speak Korean." Ironically, saying it in decent Korean is charming.
20. **도와주세요!** — *dowajuseyo* — "Help me, please!" For emergencies — along with the numbers in our [emergency guide](/health-safety/korea-emergency-numbers).

## Honorable mentions

**아이스 아메리카노 주세요** (*aiseu amerikano juseyo*) — the national beverage order, essential kit for [cafe culture](/culture-etiquette/korean-cafe-culture). **건배!** (*geonbae*) — "cheers!" **사장님** (*sajangnim*) — "boss," the friendly way to address a shop owner.

## How to use this list

Screenshot it. Practice the big five out loud once — actually out loud — before landing. Deploy #2 relentlessly. When a phrase fails, Papago picks up the pieces; but you'll be surprised how rarely it has to.
