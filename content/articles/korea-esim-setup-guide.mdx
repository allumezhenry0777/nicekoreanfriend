An eSIM is the single easiest way to arrive in Korea already connected — no airport queue, no fumbling with tiny cards. The whole thing takes about ten minutes at your kitchen table. The trick is doing it in the right order, because a rushed install is the number one reason people land in Seoul with a dead data plan.

## Before you start

Confirm three things first.

- **Your phone supports eSIM.** Most iPhones since the XR/XS and Samsung Galaxy S20 and newer do. A few models sold in Hong Kong or mainland China do not. On iPhone, if Settings, Cellular shows an "Add eSIM" option, you are good.
- **Your phone is carrier-unlocked.** A phone locked to your home carrier may refuse a foreign eSIM. If you bought it outright or finished your contract, it is almost certainly unlocked.
- **You will have stable WiFi at install time.** The profile downloads over the internet, so install at home, not on airplane WiFi or a crowded airport network.

## What you need

- Your phone, charged, with an eSIM-ready plan purchased.
- A clear photo of your **passport** (sellers require it for identity verification).
- The **QR code** or activation details the seller emails you — screenshot it immediately as a backup.
- A payment method for the purchase (about ₩10,000–30,000 for one to two weeks of unlimited data).

Buy from a Korean-focused provider if you want a bundled Korean number (Chingu Mobile and similar run on the big three networks), or a global app like Airalo for pure convenience. Data-only plans are cheaper but give you no phone number.

## Step-by-step

1. **Buy the eSIM a day or two before departure.** Upload your passport if asked. Wait for the email with your QR code and save a screenshot.
2. **Connect to home WiFi.** This matters — the install fails without a solid connection.
3. **Open the eSIM screen.** On iPhone: Settings, Cellular (or Mobile Data), Add eSIM, Use QR Code. On Samsung: Settings, Connections, SIM Manager, Add eSIM, Scan carrier QR code.
4. **Scan the QR code** (or enter the activation code manually if provided). The profile downloads in under a minute.
5. **Label the line.** Name it something like "Korea" so you can tell it apart from your home line.
6. **Do NOT set it as your data line yet.** Leave your home line active for now. Turn OFF data roaming on your home line so it cannot rack up charges.
7. **When you land in Korea,** go back into settings, set the Korea eSIM as your Mobile Data line, and turn ON data roaming *for that eSIM only*. It connects to a Korean network within a minute or two.

That is it. Airplane mode during the flight will not undo the install — the profile stays put.

## What it costs

Korean eSIMs are cheap for what they do. Ballpark unlimited-data pricing lands around ₩10,000–30,000 for one to two weeks, with 30-day options higher. Global apps cost more for unlimited — Airalo's 30-day unlimited plan is roughly US$62 (about ₩85,000) — but offer smaller capped plans (say 5GB) for less if you are a light user. Buying online is typically about 10% cheaper than an airport counter, and you skip the line.

## Common problems (and fixes)

- **"eSIM cannot be added" / download fails.** Almost always weak WiFi. Move closer to the router and retry. Do not keep hammering the button — the code may be single-use.
- **Installed but no data after landing.** You forgot to set the eSIM as the data line and enable roaming *for it*. Fix both in settings.
- **QR says "already used."** It was scanned once already; reinstalling a used code usually will not work. Contact the seller for a reissue.
- **Phone locked to home carrier.** A foreign eSIM will not connect. You will need to unlock the phone or fall back to a [physical SIM at the airport](/phone-internet/korea-prepaid-sim-guide).
- **No Korean number.** Expected on data-only plans. If you need one for bookings and [taxi apps](/getting-around/korea-taxi-apps), buy a plan that includes a number.

## Good to know

Your eSIM data plan usually starts its countdown the moment it first touches a Korean network, not when you install it — so installing early at home is safe. Keep that backup screenshot of the QR until your trip ends.
