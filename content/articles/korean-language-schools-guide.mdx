"Just watch dramas" gets you surprisingly far in Korean — and then it stops, usually right around the moment you need to say something yourself. If you're serious enough to study *in* Korea, you have two very different roads: the university language institutes with their famous acronyms, or the private hagwon (학원) world. They differ in cost, pace, visa power and personality. Here's the honest comparison.

## The university programs: the immersion machine

Five Seoul institutes dominate the conversation, and they share a format: **10-week terms, four hours of class a day, five days a week, six levels** from absolute beginner to near-fluent. Four intakes a year (spring, summer, fall, winter). It is a genuine full-time commitment — with homework, closer to a job.

As of 2026, tuition per 10-week term runs:

- **Yonsei KLI** — about ₩1,860,000. The most famous name, a grammar-centric curriculum, excellent TOPIK preparation, huge class availability.
- **Sogang KLEC** — about ₩1,830,000. The speaking-first program; you talk from day one. Graduates are the confident conversationalists of the bunch.
- **SNU (Seoul National University)** — about ₩1,590,000, the most affordable of the big names, with a balanced method and the SNU name on your certificate.
- **Ewha** — about ₩1,710,000. Grammar-and-reading leaning with a slightly gentler curve.
- **Korea University** — about ₩1,710,000. Balanced curriculum, big pleasant campus.

Add these up honestly: a year (four terms) is ₩6.5–7.5 million in tuition alone, before Seoul rent. This is the expensive road. It is also, by a wide margin, the fastest — full-time students routinely go from zero to holding real conversations in under a year.

**The visa is the other half of the deal.** Enroll and pay for **two or more consecutive terms (20+ weeks)** and the university sponsors a **D-4 trainee visa** — your legal basis for actually living here, getting an [ARC](/living-in-korea/arc-residence-card-guide), a [bank account](/money-banking/open-bank-account-korea) and a [real phone plan](/phone-internet/korea-phone-plan-residents). A single term can often be done on visa-free entry if your nationality allows; anything longer needs the D-4. Note that D-4 students face attendance requirements — skip too many classes and your visa renewal gets awkward — and part-time work is allowed only with a permit and usually only after your first six months ([the rules](/work-study-visas/student-part-time-work-korea)).

Which one to pick? Match the method to your goal. Aiming at TOPIK and eventually a degree here: Yonsei or SNU. Want to *speak*: Sogang. Want the balanced default: SNU, which is also the price pick. In truth, your classmates and your own consistency matter more than the badge — nobody has ever failed to learn Korean because they chose Ewha over Yonsei.

## The hagwon road: flexible and half the price

Private language hagwons cluster in Gangnam and Hongdae, and universities aside, this is where Koreans themselves learn *their* foreign languages, so the industry is mature. Group classes typically run a few hundred thousand won per month for a few sessions a week — **roughly half the per-hour cost of university programs, with a tenth of the commitment**.

What you gain: schedule flexibility (evening and weekend classes exist), smaller commitments, faster enrollment, often more conversational focus. What you lose: the intensity that makes immersion work, the university certificate, and — decisively for many readers — **the visa. Hagwons essentially never sponsor D-4s.** A hagwon is the right answer if you're already in Korea on another status: a working holiday, a [digital nomad visa](/work-study-visas/korea-digital-nomad-visa), a working E visa, or a spouse visa.

There's also the third road nobody budgets for: language exchange meetups, tutoring apps, and the free or near-free classes some district offices and community centers run. Slower, sociable, and an excellent supplement — just not a structure that gets anyone to fluency alone.

## The honest decision table

**Choose a university program if**: you want to live in Korea *for* the language, need the D-4 visa to do it, can afford roughly ₩10 million+ for a year all-in, and thrive with structure. This is the road to TOPIK 4+ and everything it unlocks — university admission, better jobs, stronger visa points.

**Choose a hagwon if**: you're already here on another visa, you're working or traveling alongside study, or you want to test your commitment for three months before betting a year on it.

**Whichever you choose**: show up daily, and get your survival phrases running early — our [Korean phrases guide](/culture-etiquette/korean-phrases-for-travelers) covers the first week's worth, and understanding [Korean age and honorifics](/culture-etiquette/korean-age-and-honorifics) will make your classroom Korean land much better in the wild. The classroom teaches you the language; the convenience store, the [food ordering](/food-dining/how-to-order-korean-restaurant), and your new friends teach you the country.
