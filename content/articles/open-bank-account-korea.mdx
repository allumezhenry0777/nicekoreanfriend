Opening a Korean bank account is one of those tasks that's genuinely easy *once you have the right documents* — and genuinely frustrating if you show up missing one. The make-or-break item is almost always your Alien Registration Card. Here's exactly what to bring, where to go, and how to avoid walking out with a crippled starter account.

## Before you start

The single biggest requirement is your **ARC (Alien Registration Card)**. Nearly every bank needs it to open a normal account, because it verifies your legal stay and visa type. If you don't have it yet, read our [ARC guide](/living-in-korea/arc-residence-card-guide) first — getting the card is usually step one of settling in.

You'll also want a **Korean phone number** before you go. Mobile banking, one-time passwords, and identity verification all hang off a local 010 number, so sort that out first (see our [prepaid SIM guide](/phone-internet/korea-prepaid-sim-guide)). And choose a **foreigner-friendly branch** — one with an English-speaking global desk — rather than a random neighborhood branch where staff may not be set up to help.

## What you need

Bring **originals**, not photos or copies:

- **Alien Registration Card (ARC)** — the key document. Since March 21, 2025, several banks also accept the **mobile ARC** on your phone: Shinhan, Hana, iM, Busan, Jeonbuk, and Jeju were the first to do so.
- **Passport** — for identity, alongside the ARC.
- **Korean phone number** — registered in your name.
- **Proof of purpose** — an employment contract, employment certificate, lease agreement, university enrollment letter, or a recent utility bill. This is what lets you skip the limited-account trap (below).

## Step-by-step

1. **Call the branch first.** Confirm they open accounts for foreigners, that they have English support, and which ID they'll accept. Five minutes on the phone saves a wasted trip.
2. **Go in person, early.** Account opening is done at the counter, and it takes 30–60 minutes. Mornings are quieter; avoid lunch hours and the end of the month.
3. **Take a number and ask for the global/foreigner desk.** Hand over your ARC, passport, phone number, and proof of purpose.
4. **Choose your account and ask the four key questions:** Which ID will you accept? Will this open as a limited-transaction account? What are the daily transfer and ATM limits? What document lifts the limit?
5. **Set up your card and app.** Request a debit (check) card — often printed on the spot — and have staff help you register the mobile banking app and internet-banking authentication before you leave.
6. **Test it.** Do a small transfer or ATM withdrawal that day to confirm your card, PIN, and app all work.

## What it costs

Opening the account itself is typically **free**, and there's usually no minimum deposit beyond a token amount. A debit card is generally free to issue.

The ongoing costs are small: modest fees for transfers made outside your own bank's network or ATMs, and international remittance fees if you send money home. Your deposits are protected by law up to **₩100,000,000 per person, per bank** (raised effective September 1, 2025), which comfortably covers most people's balances.

## Common problems (and fixes)

- **You get a "limited-transaction account" (한도제한계좌 — hando-jehan gyejwa).** Opened when you can't prove why you need the account, it caps daily transfers and ATM withdrawals at around ₩1,000,000. **Fix:** bring proof of purpose — a job contract, lease, enrollment letter, or utility bill — and ask to have the limit removed.
- **The branch says no.** Some branches are simply less experienced with foreigners. **Fix:** try a branch in an international district (Itaewon, Hongdae, near a major university) or a bank known for foreigner services.
- **No ARC yet.** **Fix:** a few branches will open a basic account on your passport plus extra documents, with tighter limits, until your ARC arrives — call ahead to find one, or use a prepaid card meanwhile.
- **Name mismatch.** Your bank, phone, and ARC names must match exactly. A discrepancy (middle name, spelling) can block verification. **Fix:** make sure your phone registration matches your ARC before you apply.

## Good to know

- **KEB Hana** is widely regarded as the most foreigner-friendly, with strong English app support and dedicated global branches; **Shinhan**, **Woori**, and **KB Kookmin** are also solid choices with English service.
- **Bring your own translator if unsure.** Even at global desks, a Korean-speaking friend smooths out edge cases.
- **Set up mobile banking on the spot.** It's far easier to have staff walk you through app registration and authentication in person than to fight with it alone later.
