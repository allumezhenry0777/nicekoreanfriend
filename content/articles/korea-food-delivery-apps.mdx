Short answer: yes, foreigners can order delivery in Korea, and it's genuinely one of the best delivery ecosystems on the planet — fast, cheap, and it'll bring a single bowl of soup to your door at 2am. The catch used to be that the apps assumed a Korean phone number and a Korean card. That's largely fixed now. Here's how the three big apps actually behave for someone who just landed.

## The short version

There are three players: **배달의민족 (Baedal-ui-minjok — "Baemin")**, **쿠팡이츠 (Coupang Eats)**, and **요기요 (Yogiyo)**. For a foreigner, **Baemin is the default pick**: it has a proper in-app English mode, doesn't demand a Korean phone number, and takes overseas cards. **Coupang Eats** is a strong second with an English interface and tends to be quick. **Yogiyo** is the most Korean-only of the three and the hardest to use without help. All three let you find your address by map pin, so you never have to type Hangul.

## How it actually works

The flow is the same across apps: set your delivery location, browse nearby restaurants, add items to a cart, pay in-app, and track the rider on a live map. A few things worth knowing:

- **Language.** Baemin's app supports **English, Japanese, and Chinese** in its settings. Coupang Eats offers an English interface. Yogiyo is **mostly Korean** — the workaround is opening the Yogiyo website in Chrome and letting Google Translate render the menus, then ordering there.
- **Sign-up.** Baemin notably does **not require a Korean phone number**, which is the wall that stops a lot of newcomers on other services. You can register with an email and get going.
- **Address.** Search or drop a pin. If you're in a hotel or Airbnb, pin the building and add the room number in the delivery-notes field (a translation app helps for the note).
- **Menus.** Even in English mode, individual restaurant menus are often still in Korean, since they're entered by the shops. Photos help; so does our guide to [Korean dishes for beginners](/food-dining/korean-dishes-for-beginners).

## What it costs

Delivery pricing has three parts — the food, a minimum order, and a delivery fee:

- **Minimum order:** usually **₩12,000–15,000** per restaurant. Below that, you can't check out.
- **Delivery fee:** **₩0–5,000**, driven mostly by distance and the restaurant's own policy. It's shown before you pay.
- **Single-portion exception:** ordering for one used to mean padding your cart to hit the minimum. Now **Baemin's 한그릇 (han-geureut — "one bowl")** category has **no minimum**, and **Coupang Eats launched a comparable no-minimum single-serving option in August 2025.** Great for solo travelers.

Payment: overseas **Visa/Mastercard** work on Baemin, which also added **Apple Pay with overseas cards** in 2025. Coupang Eats accepts foreign cards as well, though whether a given card sails through can depend on your issuer. If a card is refused, having a backup card — or a Korean bank card once you're set up — solves it.

## Common misconceptions

- **"You need an Alien Registration Card (ARC)."** You don't. A passport-holding tourist can order; the barrier was always the phone number and card, not residency.
- **"You must read Korean."** English mode plus map-pin addressing gets you most of the way. Photos and a translation app cover the rest.
- **"Delivery only comes to houses."** Hotels, guesthouses, offices, and even some parks work — just pin the location and leave a clear note.
- **"It's expensive."** Between low minimums, frequent free-delivery promos, and no tipping, a solo meal often lands cheaper than a sit-down restaurant.

## What locals do

Koreans treat delivery as everyday infrastructure, not a splurge. A few habits worth stealing: they **compare the same restaurant across apps**, because fees and promos differ; they lean on **Baemin for selection** and **Coupang Eats for speed**; and they order **single-portion** items without a second thought thanks to the no-minimum categories. Many also keep the app's map note pre-filled with door codes and "leave at the door" instructions. And nobody tips — the price in the app is the price you pay.
