If your phone does not do eSIM, or you just prefer a physical card you can swap and forget, a prepaid tourist SIM is the straightforward move. You can have one working before you leave the airport, and the staff do the fiddly part for you. Here is exactly how it goes.

## Before you start

A prepaid SIM makes sense when: your phone has no eSIM support, your phone is carrier-unlocked, and you are staying a few days to a few weeks. If you are staying longer than about three months, this is only a stopgap until your residence card lets you get a cheaper monthly plan — see our [guide for residents](/phone-internet/korea-phone-plan-residents).

One thing to sort out at home: make sure your phone is **unlocked**. A handset still locked to your home carrier will reject a Korean SIM, and there is nothing the counter staff can do about it.

## What you need

- Your **passport** — required by law for activation, no exceptions.
- An **unlocked** phone with a physical SIM slot.
- The **SIM ejector tool** (or a paperclip) to open the tray.
- A payment method — cards are accepted at airport counters.
- A safe spot for your **home SIM** once it comes out. A small case or even the SIM card packaging works; losing it is the classic rookie mistake.

## Step-by-step

1. **Clear immigration and baggage,** then head into the arrivals hall.
2. **Find a carrier counter.** KT (olleh), SK Telecom, and LG U+ all have booths in the arrivals halls of both Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 at Incheon. Some booths run 24 hours; others open roughly 6–7am to 10pm.
3. **Tell them your plan and duration** — for example, "unlimited data, 10 days." Staff speak enough English for the transaction.
4. **Hand over your passport** for the required identity check.
5. **Let them insert and activate the SIM.** They pop out your home SIM, insert the Korean one, and confirm data is live before you walk away. Watch where your home SIM goes.
6. **Test it on the spot** — open a maps app and confirm you have data before leaving the counter.

If you pre-ordered online, look for the pickup desk named in your confirmation; you show a voucher and passport and skip most of the setup.

## What it costs

Prices are similar across all three carriers. At Incheon, unlimited-data tourist SIMs run about:

- **3 days:** ₩18,000
- **5 days:** ₩27,500
- **10 days:** ₩38,500
- **30 days:** ₩71,500

Shorter 1-day options exist (around ₩6,600), and 90-day plans run near ₩143,000. Two things to know about calls: most tourist SIMs let you *receive* calls and texts free, but *making* calls needs a voice top-up of around ₩5,500; and some "data-only" SIMs come with no number at all. If you want a Korean number for restaurant bookings or [taxi apps](/getting-around/korea-taxi-apps), confirm that before paying. Pre-ordering online for airport pickup is generally about 10% cheaper.

## Common problems (and fixes)

- **SIM will not activate.** Usually a locked phone. Confirm your handset is carrier-unlocked before your trip.
- **You lost your home SIM.** Prevent it: put it straight into a case the moment it comes out. If lost, your home carrier can reissue one when you return.
- **No signal after leaving the counter.** Go back before you walk off — that is why you test on the spot. Often it just needs a restart or a manual carrier selection.
- **Wrong tray size.** Modern SIMs punch out to nano size; the staff handle this, but keep the surrounding plastic frame in case you need a bigger format later.
- **Cannot make calls.** Expected on data-first plans. Add the voice top-up, or just use data apps like KakaoTalk for calls.

## Good to know

A physical SIM is reliable and dead simple, but it does tie up your only SIM slot, which is the main reason eSIM has taken over for newer phones — compare them in our [eSIM vs SIM vs pocket WiFi guide](/phone-internet/esim-vs-sim-vs-pocket-wifi-korea), or read the [eSIM setup steps](/phone-internet/korea-esim-setup-guide) if your phone supports it. Either way, the first thing to do once you are connected is install the [essential apps for Korea](/first-72-hours/essential-apps-korea) so maps, translation, and messaging are ready before you head into the city.
