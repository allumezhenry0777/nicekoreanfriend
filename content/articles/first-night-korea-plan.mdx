You've been on a plane for ten-plus hours, it's dark outside, and every sign is in a script you can't read yet. Breathe. Your first night in Korea only has four real jobs: get online, get a transit card, get some cash, and get to bed. Everything else can wait until morning. Here's the calm, in-order version — including what to do if you land after the last train.

## Before you start

Do two things while you're still on the plane or in the immigration line. First, screenshot your accommodation's address in Korean (Korean drivers and maps love the Korean address, not the romanized one). Second, decide how you're getting online: an **eSIM** you activate the second you land, or a **physical SIM** you buy at an airport counter. If your phone supports eSIM, setting it up before you fly is the least stressful option — you walk out already connected.

Also know your arrival time honestly. The **Express AREX train** to Seoul Station makes its last run around **22:40**, and the all-stop train a little later (roughly 23:30). If your flight lands after that, you're on the night-bus-or-taxi plan, which is totally fine — just good to know before you're standing in the arrivals hall.

## What you need

- **Your passport** — required to buy a SIM (no ARC or Korean address needed).
- **A payment card that works abroad** — Visa/Mastercard are widely accepted, plus a card that can pull cash from a **Global ATM**.
- **A little cash, or a plan to get it** — some counters and top-ups prefer won.
- **A maps app that works in Korea** — Naver Map or KakaoMap. Google Maps directions are limited here. See our [essential apps guide](/first-72-hours/essential-apps-korea).
- **Your accommodation address saved in Korean.**

## Step-by-step

**1. Get connected.** In the Arrivals hall, SK Telecom, KT, and LG U+ all have counters (at least one runs 24 hours in each terminal). Hand over your passport; a tourist data plan runs roughly **₩30,000–60,000** depending on days and data. If you pre-bought an eSIM, just switch it on now.

**2. Pull cash.** Find a **Global ATM** (look for the "Global" sticker). Withdraw ₩50,000–100,000 to cover the first night. Convenience-store ATMs charge about **₩3,000–3,600** per withdrawal, so take enough in one go rather than several small pulls. More on this in [cash vs card in Korea](/money-banking/cash-vs-card-korea).

**3. Grab a T-money card.** Buy one at any convenience store or the machines near the station (about **₩2,500–5,000** for the card itself), then load it with **cash** in ₩1,000 units — ₩10,000–20,000 is plenty for night one. This one card works on the subway, buses, and even taxis. Full details in our [T-money card guide](/getting-around/t-money-card-guide).

**4. Get to your bed.** If trains are still running, the AREX is fast and cheap. If you've missed them, take an **airport night bus** (N6000/N6001/N6002, about **₩9,000**, running roughly 23:30–04:00) or a **taxi** (about **₩75,000–90,000** to Seoul, plus a 20% surcharge midnight–4am). See [Incheon to Seoul transport](/first-72-hours/incheon-to-seoul-transport) for the routes.

**5. Eat something.** Near your accommodation, duck into a 24-hour convenience store. A hot bowl of ramyeon, a gimbap roll, and water will run you well under **₩10,000** and you can microwave it right there.

## What it costs

A realistic first-night budget, arriving late:

- SIM/eSIM data plan: **₩30,000–60,000** (or less pre-bought)
- ATM withdrawal fee: **₩3,000–3,600**
- T-money card + top-up: **₩12,500–25,000**
- Night bus or taxi: **₩9,000** (bus) to **~₩90,000+** (late taxi)
- Convenience-store dinner: **under ₩10,000**

So somewhere between **₩60,000 and ₩190,000** depending mostly on whether you bus or taxi.

## Common problems (and fixes)

- **"My card was declined at the SIM counter."** Try another card, or pay cash — that's exactly why you pulled won first.
- **"The ATM rejected my card."** Not every ATM takes foreign cards. Look specifically for the **Global ATM** label; convenience-store machines are a reliable fallback.
- **"I missed the last train."** No panic — walk to the night-bus stand outside, or the taxi rank. Both run through the small hours.
- **"Everything's closed."** Some convenience stores now shut roughly **1am–6am**. If one's dark, the next block usually has another; 24-hour branches cluster near stations and hotels.
- **"I can't read the taxi app."** KakaoTaxi has an English mode; or show the driver your saved Korean address on screen.

## Good to know

Tap water is safe to drink, but most people drink bottled or filtered — grab a bottle for your room. Don't tip the taxi driver or anyone else; it's simply not expected here and can cause confusion. And genuinely, resist the urge to fix your whole trip tonight. Sleep off the jet lag, and handle banking, longer SIM plans, and sightseeing tomorrow — see the [first-day mistakes](/first-72-hours/korea-first-day-mistakes) to sidestep so morning-you starts smoother.
