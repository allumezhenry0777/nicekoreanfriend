Korea runs on data. Maps, taxis, translation, restaurant menus, subway apps — almost nothing works well offline. So before you land, you have one real decision to make: eSIM, physical SIM, or pocket WiFi. Here is the honest version, without the affiliate-link hype.

## The quick verdict

If you have a fairly recent phone and you are traveling solo or as a couple who each carry a phone, buy a **Korean eSIM online before you fly**. It is the cheapest, fastest, and least fiddly option — you install it at home and walk out of the airport already connected. Expect roughly ₩10,000–30,000 for one to two weeks of unlimited data.

Pick a **physical SIM** if your phone does not support eSIM, or if you specifically want a Korean phone number and prefer swapping a card to fiddling with QR codes.

Pick **pocket WiFi** (a rental router, often called a "WiFi egg" or 에그 — egeu) only when a group of three or more wants to share a single connection, or when you are carrying gadgets that have no SIM slot at all.

## Option by option

### eSIM — the default for most people

An eSIM is a digital SIM you download instead of slotting in. You buy it from a Korean provider or a global app, receive a QR code, and install it while still at home on WiFi. When you land in Korea, it switches on by itself. Your original SIM stays in the phone, so your home number keeps receiving texts.

Korean-focused sellers like Chingu Mobile run on the big three networks and often bundle a Korean number; global apps like Airalo are convenient but pricier for unlimited data (their 30-day unlimited plan is around US$62, roughly ₩85,000). The catch is compatibility: most iPhones since the XR/XS and Samsung Galaxy S20 and newer are fine, but a handful of phones sold in Hong Kong or mainland China have no eSIM at all. Check first. Our [step-by-step eSIM setup guide](/phone-internet/korea-esim-setup-guide) walks through the install.

### Physical SIM — the reliable fallback

A physical SIM is the old-fashioned card. You can order one for airport pickup or buy it at a carrier counter after you land. It is the right call if your phone is eSIM-incompatible, if you want to keep things simple, or if you want a Korean number for things like [taxi apps](/getting-around/korea-taxi-apps) and bookings. The downside: you have to physically swap cards (bring the little ejector tool), and your home SIM comes out and can get lost. See our [prepaid SIM walkthrough](/phone-internet/korea-prepaid-sim-guide) for exactly what to do at the counter.

### Pocket WiFi — only for sharing

A pocket WiFi is a pocket-sized router you rent, charge daily, and carry everywhere. Its one genuine strength is sharing: three or four people can connect at once, which splits the daily rental into something cheap per person. But you have to keep it charged, remember to bring it, and return it before you fly home — and if it dies, everyone loses signal at once. For a solo traveler it is simply one more thing to babysit.

## Costs compared

| Option | Real-world price | What you actually pay for |
|---|---|---|
| Korean eSIM | ~₩10,000–30,000 for 1–2 weeks unlimited | Data only, instant, no counter |
| Airport/physical SIM | 3 days ~₩18,000 · 5 days ~₩27,500 · 10 days ~₩38,500 · 30 days ~₩71,500 | Unlimited data; some include a number |
| Pocket WiFi | ~₩3,500–8,000 per day + refundable deposit | One shared connection for a group |

Two money notes. First, pre-ordering an eSIM or SIM online is often about 10% cheaper than buying at the airport counter, and it saves you queuing after a long flight. Second, most tourist SIMs give you unlimited *data* but let you only *receive* calls — making outbound calls needs a voice top-up (around ₩5,500). If you rely on data apps instead of phone calls, that rarely matters.

## Which should you choose?

- **Solo or a couple, recent phone:** Korean eSIM. Cheapest, zero counter time, connected on landing.
- **Phone has no eSIM, or you want a Korean number:** physical SIM, ideally pre-ordered for airport pickup.
- **Group of 3+ sharing, or you have SIM-less gadgets:** pocket WiFi.
- **Long-term resident (90+ days):** none of these — once your ARC arrives, move to a monthly plan. See [phone plans for residents](/phone-internet/korea-phone-plan-residents).

Whatever you choose, set it up before or immediately on arrival — your first hour in Korea goes much better with working maps and translation. Pair it with the [essential apps to install](/first-72-hours/essential-apps-korea) and you are genuinely ready to move.
