Korea has one of the fastest, cheapest delivery systems on earth — order in the morning, unbox by night is normal. But the first few packages can be confusing: two different systems, parcel lockers with codes, and customs codes for anything from abroad. Here's the whole picture.

## The short version

Two systems handle your stuff, and they're not the same thing:

- **우체국 (ucheguk — Korea Post):** the national post office. Letters, bills, registered mail, and international shipping (EMS) go here. This is government mail.
- **택배 (taekbae — courier):** private door-to-door parcel companies — **CJ Logistics (CJ대한통운)**, Hanjin, Lotte, and others. Almost everything you buy online arrives this way, usually next-day.

When you shop on Coupang, Naver, Gmarket or similar, you're getting 택배. When you mail a document home, you're using the 우체국. Give a correct address and a Korean phone number and both work beautifully.

## How it actually works

**Your address is a road-name address (도로명주소).** Write the full road name, the building number, then your unit/apartment number, plus the 5-digit postal code. In shopping apps you just search your building and pick your unit — the app fills in the rest. The single most important extra: **add a Korean mobile number** so the driver can call or text you.

Once a parcel is out for delivery, it lands in one of three places:

1. **At your door.** The default for houses and villas. Drivers often leave it if you're out.
2. **In a 무인택배함 (muin-taekbaeham — parcel locker).** Common in apartments and officetels: the courier locks it in a lobby compartment and texts you a code to open it. If the code fails, ask the management office (관리사무소) or security desk.
3. **At a convenience store (편의점 택배 pickup).** You can choose store pickup at checkout — handy if you're rarely home. You get a text, then collect it from the counter with your name and code.

To track it, most shopping apps show live status inside the app. For anything else, the carrier's own site or a tracking aggregator app works with your invoice number (송장번호). Getting your phone and payment set up first makes all of this smoother — see our [essential apps guide](/first-72-hours/essential-apps-korea).

## What it costs

- **Receiving domestic 택배:** free — the seller pays. That's why online shopping is so frictionless.
- **Sending a domestic parcel:** roughly ₩3,000–6,000 for a normal box by courier or Korea Post, depending on size and weight.
- **Convenience-store parcel pickup/drop-off:** similar, often a little cheaper for sending.
- **International EMS:** varies widely by weight and country — the Korea Post site quotes exact rates.
- **Customs on inbound overseas parcels:** duty-free for personal use under about **₩150,000 (roughly USD 150)**, or **USD 200** for goods shipped from the US. Above the threshold, expect customs duty plus **10% VAT** on the value.

## Common misconceptions

- **"택배 and the post office are the same."** They're separate. Missing a courier package? Contact the courier, not the 우체국.
- **"I can order from abroad with no paperwork."** For most overseas online orders you need a **personal clearance code (개인통관고유부호 — gaein-tonggwan-goyu-buho)**, a free ID number you generate once on the Korea Customs site. Enter it at checkout and it clears customs under your name.
- **"Splitting an order avoids customs."** Customs can combine same-day shipments to one person, so deliberately splitting to stay under the limit doesn't reliably work.
- **"Lockers hold packages forever."** Parcel lockers and store pickups have a pickup window (often a few days). Collect promptly or it may be returned.

## What locals do

Koreans lean hard on **store pickup and lockers** so they never wait home for a driver. They save their address and phone in every shopping app so checkout is one tap. For anything valuable, they use **registered delivery** or require a signature. And before buying from overseas sites, they set up their personal clearance code once and reuse it forever. If a courier can't reach you, they'll usually call the number on the label — another reason a working Korean SIM matters from week one.

## Good to know

If your Korean is shaky, Korea Post has an English phone line at 042-609-4295 (press 8 for English). When you first move in, check whether your building has a parcel locker and where the management office is — it saves a lot of confusion on your first delivery, and it pairs naturally with setting up the rest of your place (see [setting up your first apartment](/living-in-korea/first-apartment-setup-korea)). Convenience stores double as your parcel hub for sending, receiving and returns, so it's worth knowing yours — our [convenience store guide](/food-dining/korean-convenience-store-guide) covers what else they can do. Get your address and phone saved everywhere, and packages basically take care of themselves.
