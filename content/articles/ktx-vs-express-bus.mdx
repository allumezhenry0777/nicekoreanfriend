Every Korean has an instinct for this one. Going to Busan for the weekend and short on time? Train. Heading to a small beach town, or watching the budget on a longer trip? Bus. Both networks are excellent and cover the whole country — the real question is what you're optimizing for. Here's how I actually decide.

## The quick verdict

**KTX for speed, express bus for price and reach.** The train roughly halves your travel time on major routes and drops you in the city center, but costs two to three times more. The bus is cheap, leaves constantly, and — this is the part visitors miss — goes to hundreds of towns the train simply doesn't. If your destination has a KTX station and you value the afternoon, take the train. Otherwise the bus is no hardship at all.

## At a glance

| Factor | KTX (high-speed train) | Express bus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seoul → Busan time | ~2 hr 17 min | ~4–4.5 hr |
| Seoul → Busan fare | ~₩59,800 standard | ~₩24,000 standard |
| Comfort | Roomy, walk around, restroom, snack car | Reclining seats; premium is business-class |
| Reach | Major cities on the high-speed line | Almost every town, big and small |
| Frequency | Very frequent on main routes | Extremely frequent, all day |
| Departs from | Seoul / Yongsan / Suseo stations | Express Bus Terminal (Gangnam) and others |

## Option by option

### KTX

The bullet train hits up to 300 km/h and connects Seoul with Busan, Daejeon, Dongdaegu, Gwangju, Mokpo and more. You get a reserved seat, power outlets, room to stretch, and the freedom to visit the restroom or buy a snack mid-trip. Stations sit in city centers, so you arrive where you actually want to be. As of the September 2026 KTX–SRT merger, everything books through one **KORAIL+** app, and a 10% fare cut is being phased in. Full booking walkthrough in the [KTX train guide](/getting-around/ktx-train-guide).

### Express bus (고속버스, gosok-beoseu)

Korea's intercity coaches are modern, clean and astonishingly frequent — big routes leave every 10–30 minutes from early morning to late night. There are three classes:

- **Standard (일반, ilban):** four seats per row, the cheapest fare.
- **Deluxe (우등, udeung):** three seats per row, more recline and legroom.
- **Premium (프리미엄, peuriemieom):** three wide pods per row with a personal screen and near-flat recline — genuinely business-class.

Long routes include a bathroom break at a highway rest stop (휴게소, hyugeso), which are a fun snack destination in their own right. The catch is the clock and traffic: a Friday-evening jam out of Seoul can stretch the ride.

## Costs compared

Using Seoul → Busan as the benchmark:

| Class / option | Rough one-way fare | Travel time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Express bus, standard (일반) | ~₩24,000 | 4–4.5 hr |
| Express bus, deluxe (우등) | ~₩35,000 | 4–4.5 hr |
| Express bus, premium (프리미엄) | ~₩46,000–53,000 | 4–4.5 hr |
| KTX, standard class | ~₩59,800 | ~2 hr 17 min |
| KTX, first class (특실) | ~₩83,700 | ~2 hr 17 min |

Notice the premium bus narrows the price gap while staying slower — you pick it for comfort on a budget, not to save the most money. The standard bus is where the real savings are: less than half the KTX fare.

## Which should you choose?

- **You want your day back:** KTX. Two-plus hours saved each way is a whole extra afternoon in Busan.
- **You're counting won or traveling long-term:** standard express bus. It's the frugal Korean's default for good reason.
- **Your destination has no train station:** bus, no contest — think smaller coastal towns, mountain areas and inland cities off the high-speed line.
- **You want comfort without the KTX price:** premium bus. Recline the pod, put on a movie, arrive relaxed.
- **It's a holiday and trains are sold out:** buses add extra departures and rarely fully sell out — a reliable backup.

Either way, book ahead on weekends and holidays, and use a [T-money card](/getting-around/t-money-card-guide) plus [Naver or Kakao Map](/first-72-hours/essential-apps-korea) to reach the station or terminal.
