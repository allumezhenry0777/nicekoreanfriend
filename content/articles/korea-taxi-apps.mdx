Taxis in Korea are cheap, plentiful and honest — meters run straight, drivers rarely detour, and there is no haggling. The one thing that trips up visitors is payment: the app everyone uses can quietly reject your card. Here is how the system actually works and how to ride without friction.

## The short version

The market is basically one app: **Kakao T (카카오 T — Kakao Taxi)** handles roughly 90% of app-hailed rides. It works in English and you can register with a foreign number, but its in-app payment often refuses foreign cards. Two clean workarounds: use **k.ride**, Kakao Mobility's sister app made for foreign visitors (it takes overseas cards), or book on Kakao T with "pay the driver" and tap your card or T-money on the terminal in the taxi. And you can always just raise your hand on the street — hailing is still completely normal.

## How it actually works

There are three ways to get a cab, and they overlap.

- **Kakao T:** download it, switch the interface to English or Japanese in the profile/language menu, set your pickup pin and destination, and the app translates your destination into Korean for the driver, shows a fare estimate, and matches you with a nearby car. It commands about 90% of the hailing market, so a car usually arrives in a couple of minutes.
- **k.ride:** launched by Kakao Mobility specifically for international users, tapping the same driver network. You can sign up with a foreign phone number or a Google/Apple account and register an **overseas Visa, Mastercard, JCB or UnionPay** card for automatic in-app payment. This is the smoothest option if you want cashless door-to-door.
- **UT (Uber):** the Uber app in Korea dispatches licensed taxis (branded UT) in Seoul and some cities. Familiar interface, foreign cards accepted, but fewer cars than Kakao in many areas.
- **Street hailing:** an orange or silver taxi with a red 빈차 (bincha — vacant) light in the windshield is free. Wave it down, show your destination on a map, and go. No call fee.

Taxi colors are just branding — orange, silver and white are all regular metered taxis. **Black taxis are "deluxe" (모범 — mobeom):** roomier, pricier, and often with an English-speaking driver.

## What it costs

Fares are set by the meter, by city. In Seoul as of 2026:

- **Base fare:** about **₩4,800** for the first 1.6 km, then it ticks up by distance and time.
- **Night surcharge:** roughly **20–40% extra between 10pm and 4am**, with the steepest rate from 11pm to 2am.
- **Call fee:** booking through an app can add **up to ~₩3,000**, rising with demand (rush hour, late night, rain). A street hail has none.
- **Tolls:** any bridge or expressway toll is added to your fare, shown separately.

A typical short hop across a district is ₩6,000–12,000; a longer cross-city ride ₩15,000–30,000. It is one of the better taxi bargains among wealthy countries. For how card-vs-cash works more broadly, see [cash vs card in Korea](/money-banking/cash-vs-card-korea).

## Common misconceptions

- **"I have to tip."** You do not. There is no tipping culture for taxis in Korea — pay the meter (plus call fee if any) and that is it.
- **"Kakao T takes my foreign card."** Often it does not for in-app payment. Assume it won't, and plan to pay the driver directly or use k.ride.
- **"Drivers speak English."** Usually not much, and they don't need to — the app passes your destination in Korean, or you show a map pin. Deluxe (black) and international taxis are the ones with English-speaking drivers.
- **"Refusing a ride is normal."** Legally drivers shouldn't refuse based on your destination, and with app dispatch it rarely happens because they accept before seeing you.

## What locals do

Koreans live in Kakao T. They register a Korean card, hail with two taps, and pay automatically — the call fee is just accepted as the price of not waiting. For short distances in a busy area, plenty still flag one down on the street to skip the fee. Late at night, when demand spikes and the surcharge kicks in, everyone books ahead through the app rather than fighting for a street cab. Copy that rhythm: app when it's busy or you're far out, street hail when a vacant taxi is right there.

## Good to know

- **Keep T-money loaded.** Tapping a T-money card in the taxi is the most reliable cashless payment for foreigners — it sidesteps the foreign-card problem entirely. See the [T-money card guide](/getting-around/t-money-card-guide).
- **Screenshot your destination in Korean.** The app does this for you, but for a street hail, having the Korean name or a map pin ready removes all doubt.
- **Buckle up in back.** Rear seatbelts are required and drivers may remind you.
- **Airport runs are their own thing.** From Incheon, taxis add a ₩7,900 expressway toll and there are flat-fare international taxis — we cover that in [Incheon Airport to Seoul compared](/first-72-hours/incheon-to-seoul-transport).
- **Get the app before you need it.** Install and set the language on hotel WiFi, not curbside in the rain. Our [essential apps guide](/first-72-hours/essential-apps-korea) lists the rest.
