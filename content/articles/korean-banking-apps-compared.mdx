Every Korean uses a banking app for daily life, and as a foreigner you'll want one too. The catch: the apps Koreans rave about aren't all open to you. The famous "easy" digital banks are built around a Korean national ID, while the app that actually accepts your ARC gets less hype. Here's the honest comparison.

## The quick verdict

If you want a **simple digital account you can open from your couch**, go with **Toss Bank** — it accepts ARC holders online. If you want **full features with English support and a human to help when things break**, use a **traditional bank** (Shinhan, Hana, or Woori) and its app. Treat **KakaoBank** as probably-not-available: its standard signup has required a Korean national ID and rejected the ARC.

Whichever you pick, you'll need an ARC and a Korean phone number first — no app gets around that.

| App / Bank | Opens with just an ARC? | English support | How you open it | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Toss Bank** | Yes, online | Mostly Korean UI | In-app, no branch needed | An easy all-in-one digital account |

## Option by option

### Toss Bank

The standout for foreigners who want a digital account. ARC holders can open Toss online — it accepts the Alien Registration Card, permanent residency card, or domestic residence card as valid ID. Account, debit card, transfers, and payments all live in one clean app. The interface is primarily Korean, so keep a translation tool handy during setup, and make sure your name matches exactly across your ARC and SIM registration or verification will fail. For most independent arrivals, this is the fastest path to "I have a working Korean account."

### KakaoBank

Wildly popular with locals, but its normal signup has required a Korean national ID (주민등록번호 — jumin deungnok beonho) and turned down the 외국인등록증 (oegugin deungnokjeung — ARC). The English homepage is informational and doesn't change eligibility. Policies can evolve, so it's worth a look in-app — but don't build your plan around it.

### Shinhan SOL

Shinhan's app has a genuine English interface and the bank runs foreigner-focused services and global desks. App-based opening can carry conditions (certain visa types and a minimum time worked in Korea), so many foreigners open at a branch and then use SOL for everything after. A great all-rounder if English usability matters.

### Hana (Hana 1Q)

KEB Hana is repeatedly ranked the most foreigner-friendly bank, with English app support and a dense network of global branches staffed by multilingual tellers. If you value being able to walk into a branch and get help in English, Hana is the safe pick. See our full walkthrough on [opening an account](/money-banking/open-bank-account-korea).

### Woori WON

Woori's app supports English and Simplified Chinese and covers all the standard banking you'd expect. English support is a notch below Shinhan and Hana, but branches are generally cooperative with the standard documents. A solid, no-drama choice.

## Costs compared

| App / Bank | Account/card cost | Everyday transfers | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Toss Bank** | Free to open, free debit card | Free within-app; low domestic fees | Fewer physical branches for in-person help |
| **KakaoBank** | Free (if eligible) | Low fees | Eligibility is the real barrier, not price |
| **Shinhan SOL** | Free/low; free debit card | Free at own ATMs; small out-of-network fees | English support + global desk included |
| **Hana / Woori** | Free/low; free debit card | Free at own ATMs; small out-of-network fees | Best for branch help and remittances |

Across the board, opening and basic use are cheap or free — the meaningful difference is **access and support**, not monthly cost. Deposits at all of them are protected up to ₩100,000,000 per person, per bank.

## Which should you choose?

- **You want the easiest digital account and have an ARC:** start with **Toss Bank**.
- **You want English support and human backup:** open at **Hana** or **Shinhan** and use their app.
- **You're tempted by KakaoBank because friends love it:** check eligibility, but expect to be turned away without a Korean national ID — have a backup ready.
- **You haven't got your ARC yet:** sort that first with our [ARC guide](/living-in-korea/arc-residence-card-guide); no app will fully onboard you without it.

Many foreigners end up running two apps: a traditional bank for salary, rent, and remittances, plus Toss for quick everyday payments.
