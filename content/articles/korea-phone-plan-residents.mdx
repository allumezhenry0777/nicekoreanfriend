If Korea is now home, not a trip, tourist SIMs stop making sense fast. Paying ₩70,000 a month to keep renewing an unlimited travel SIM is money on fire. What you want is a real resident plan — and for most foreigners, the smart choice is a budget MVNO. Here is how the system actually works.

## The short version

Korea has three big networks: SK Telecom, KT, and LG U+. You can buy directly from them, but you will usually overpay. Instead, dozens of smaller carriers called **알뜰폰 (altteulpon — "budget phone")** rent the exact same network capacity and resell it far cheaper. Same towers, same coverage, roughly 30–60% lower bills. To sign up for a proper monthly plan you need two things: your **Alien Registration Card (ARC)** and a **Korean bank account or card** for automatic payment.

## How it actually works

An MVNO does not build its own network. It buys wholesale from one of the big three and sets its own prices, so a plan advertised as "LG U+ network" gives you identical signal to a full-price LG U+ customer. Plans commonly start around ₩11,000 a month and climb based on data.

The gate is identity verification. Korean telecom rules require a verified local identity for a postpaid line, which for foreigners means your ARC number plus a bank account or Korean card set up for autopay (자동이체 — jadong-iche — automatic transfer). This is why you cannot get a monthly plan the day you land — you need the card first. Getting your residence card is its own process, covered in our [ARC guide](/living-in-korea/arc-residence-card-guide), and setting up the bank account is covered in [opening a Korean bank account](/money-banking/open-bank-account-korea).

The government even runs a comparison hub, **알뜰폰Hub (mvnohub.kr)**, listing current plans across every budget operator — handy for spotting the cheapest data tier, though the site assumes some Korean.

## What it costs

Rough monthly ranges, all on the same big-three networks:

- **Light data (a few GB):** around ₩11,000–20,000
- **Large or "unlimited" data:** around ₩25,000–40,000
- **Major-carrier flagship plans:** ₩50,000+ (you are paying for brand and store service, not better signal)

Compared with a tourist SIM at roughly ₩71,500 for 30 days, a ₩20,000 MVNO plan is a dramatic drop for the same phone doing the same things. There is typically no setup fee and no contract on a SIM-only MVNO plan.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Budget MVNO means worse coverage."** No. It is literally the same network. The differences are price and customer-service polish, not signal.
- **"I have to sign a two-year contract."** Not for a SIM-only plan — those are month-to-month. Long contracts come from buying a subsidized phone, which is a separate choice.
- **"I can set it up before my ARC arrives."** Generally no for postpaid. Use a passport-based prepaid SIM as a bridge, then switch.
- **"Signing up is only possible in Korean."** Many MVNO apps and stores do assume Korean fluency, but a growing set of foreigner-focused providers handle the whole process in English. If your Korean is shaky, choose one of those.

## What locals do

Koreans are enthusiastic 알뜰폰 users precisely because the value is obvious — the same LG U+ or KT signal for half the price. A common local pattern is to buy the phone outright (or bring your own), then run a cheap SIM-only MVNO plan with no contract, rather than bundling an expensive phone and plan together at a carrier store. The mental split is simple: the **phone** is one purchase, the **plan** is another, and keeping them separate almost always saves money.

## Good to know

The clean path for a new arrival: land, grab a [prepaid tourist SIM](/phone-internet/korea-prepaid-sim-guide) or an [eSIM](/phone-internet/korea-esim-setup-guide) to stay connected, register for your ARC, open a bank account, then move to an MVNO once both are ready — usually within your first month or two. When you sign up, have your ARC, passport, and bank details on hand; the autopay setup is the step people forget. And keep your phone unlocked so you can drop in any SIM you like.
