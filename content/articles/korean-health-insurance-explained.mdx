Understanding your insurance starts with one question: **what is your own coverage start date?** A friend’s bill or a six-month rule quoted online cannot answer that for you.

## Check your enrollment route

- **Working at an insured workplace:** check enrollment with your employer. Do not assume you must wait six months.
- **Regional enrollment:** six months is a common rule, but eligible residence categories and exceptions matter.
- **Students and other special categories:** confirm your category’s start date and any reduction with NHIS or your school. Do not budget on the assumption that every foreign resident follows the same timetable.
- **Dependents:** ask whether you qualify under a family member’s coverage; it is not automatic for every relative.

Source: [NHIS guidance for foreigners](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02900m01.do).

## Can overseas insurance replace NHIS?

NHIS permits applications for exclusion in qualifying circumstances involving alternative medical coverage. Owning an overseas policy does not automatically exempt you, and neither is exclusion universally impossible. Ask NHIS what evidence applies to your enrollment route and wait for its decision rather than stop paying on your own.

Before contacting NHIS, gather your residence card, current coverage documents and any notice you received. Write down these questions:

1. What date does my coverage start?
2. Which enrollment category applies to me?
3. Am I eligible for an exclusion application or a premium reduction?
4. Which documents and deadlines apply?
5. What should I do about an existing bill while my case is reviewed?

Source: [NHIS eligibility and exclusion guidance](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02900m01.do).

## Budget from your own bill

Employee premiums are salary-based. Regional premiums can involve an assessment, a minimum and category-specific reductions. The contribution year matters, so a flat monthly figure copied from another person is a poor planning tool.

Keep the first bill and ask NHIS to explain any unexpected amount before ignoring it. When asking your employer about deductions, request the itemized payslip rather than comparing take-home pay alone.

## At the clinic or pharmacy

Bring your residence card or other accepted ID so staff can check coverage. Ask whether the proposed treatment is covered and what you will pay. A quote for a consultation does not necessarily include tests, procedures or medicines.

Useful questions to show reception:

- **건강보험 적용되나요?** — Is this covered by national health insurance?
- **검사 전에 비용을 알려 주세요.** — Please tell me the cost before the test.
- **영수증과 진료비 세부내역서를 주세요.** — Please give me a receipt and an itemized bill.

Our [doctor visit guide](/health-safety/seeing-a-doctor-in-korea) explains the clinic-to-pharmacy sequence. For an urgent medical emergency, call **119**; see [emergency numbers](/health-safety/korea-emergency-numbers).

## If you are visiting as a tourist

Arrange travel medical cover suited to your trip. Save the insurer’s assistance contact, ask what documents a claim needs and retain receipts. If you take regular medication, keep its generic name and prescription details with you; our [pharmacy guide](/health-safety/korea-pharmacy-guide) covers asking for help locally.
