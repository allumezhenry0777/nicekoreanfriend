Incheon (ICN) is one of the smoothest big airports on earth, and arrival is genuinely easy — but the first two hours go faster when you know the order of operations. Here is exactly what happens from the moment the seatbelt sign turns off, and the two or three things worth doing before you leave the building.

## Before you start

Sort your entry paperwork on the plane or before boarding, because there is nothing to buy or queue for once you land if you have it ready.

- **e-Arrival Card:** Since January 1, 2026, Korea is fully digital and the old paper arrival card no longer exists. Submit the e-Arrival Card online at e-arrivalcard.go.kr with your passport number, flight number and the address where you are staying. You can file it up to a few days before arrival — do it at home on hotel WiFi.
- **K-ETA holders are exempt:** If you hold a valid K-ETA (the travel authorization some nationalities apply for), you do not need the e-Arrival Card. That exemption runs through December 31, 2026.
- **Know your terminal:** ICN has two terminals. Terminal 2 serves Korean Air, Delta, Air France, KLM and a few partners; almost everyone else uses Terminal 1. It matters later for where you catch transport, so check your boarding pass.

## What you need

- Your passport and your e-Arrival Card confirmation (or K-ETA).
- A card or a little cash. Nothing costs money to enter, but you will want ₩30,000–50,000 handy for a SIM and transport.
- Your Seoul address written down — immigration and the e-Arrival Card both ask for it.
- A phone with your accommodation and a maps app ready. Korea uses **Naver Map** or **Kakao Map** rather than Google Maps for directions. See our [essential apps guide](/first-72-hours/essential-apps-korea) for what to install before you fly.

## Step-by-step

1. **Follow the purple "Arrival / Immigration" signs.** Signage is in English the whole way. From far gates it can be a 10–15 minute walk plus a short shuttle train inside the terminal.
2. **Clear immigration.** Join the "Foreign Passport" line. As of March 16, 2026, the Smart Entry Service was expanded so short-term visitors from 42 eligible countries can use the automated e-gates — if you see the self-service gates and qualify, they are much faster than the staffed desks. Otherwise it is a quick photo, fingerprints and a stamp.
3. **Collect your bag.** Check the screens for your flight's carousel. Free luggage carts are everywhere.
4. **Walk through customs.** If you have nothing to declare, take the green lane — you usually just hand over a customs slip or walk straight through.
5. **Exit into the arrival hall.** Now you are landside. This is where you pick up your SIM and T-money card (next section).
6. **Head to transport.** Follow signs down to the basement (B1) for the AREX train, or to the ground-floor curb (gates 4–9 area) for airport buses and taxis.

## What it costs

Entry and immigration are free. The costs come from the two pickups and your ride into town.

- **SIM / eSIM / pocket WiFi:** roughly ₩5,000–₩40,000 depending on data and days. Counters sit between the exit gates in the arrival hall and are open 24 hours. Pre-ordering online is usually cheaper than the walk-up price — see our [prepaid SIM guide](/phone-internet/korea-prepaid-sim-guide).
- **T-money card:** the blank card is about ₩3,000–4,000, then you load however much you want. Buy it at the Transit Center or any airport convenience store, and top it up at the machine before you tap in. Full details in the [T-money card guide](/getting-around/t-money-card-guide).
- **Getting to Seoul:** the AREX all-stop train is about ₩9,500 and the express is ₩13,000; airport buses run ₩17,000–18,000; a metered taxi is roughly ₩55,000–80,000 plus a ₩7,900 expressway toll. We break down which to pick in [Incheon Airport to Seoul: AREX, bus or taxi compared](/first-72-hours/incheon-to-seoul-transport).

## Common problems (and fixes)

- **"I forgot the e-Arrival Card."** You can still fill it on your phone in the immigration hall using the free airport WiFi (network "AIRPORT WiFi Free"), or a staff member will point you to a kiosk. Do it before you reach the desk.
- **Long immigration lines.** Peak crunch is late morning and early evening when banks of flights land together. If your line is crawling, look for the automated e-gates; eligible passport holders skip most of the wait.
- **My eSIM won't activate.** Activate on the airport WiFi before you leave the building, not after. If it fails, the counter staff can help on the spot — easier than troubleshooting on the train.
- **Wrong side of the airport.** If you took the free inter-terminal shuttle to the "wrong" terminal by mistake, note it only runs one way on some loops; ask staff rather than doubling back blindly.

## Good to know

- **You need almost no Korean.** Signs, immigration and the transport counters all work in English. A few polite phrases still go a long way, but nothing here requires them.
- **Money:** ATMs marked "Global" in the arrival hall take foreign cards, and exchange counters are fine for a small top-up (rates are better in the city). Korea is very card-friendly, so you do not need much cash.
- **Last trains and buses:** the AREX and most airport buses stop running late at night. If you land after roughly midnight, a taxi may be your only option, and the night surcharge applies.
- **Terminal 2 travelers:** your AREX, bus stops and taxi ranks are all clearly signed and slightly less crowded than Terminal 1 — the process is identical.
